Slab, crawlspace or basement: the first question for single-story homes

Many single-story homes of this era sit on a concrete slab or over a crawlspace. That matters because the first sign of a hidden leak is often indirect: a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, or a water bill that climbs without a reason.

A slab leak and a crawlspace leak call for different tools, and both start with finding the water. Leak detection is the first step before anyone cuts into a floor. If you can reach the crawlspace safely, a flashlight check of the pipes under the house tells a plumber a great deal on the phone.

Storm water is a separate question from plumbing

Outdoor drainage, ditches and storm sewers are a city and property matter. A plumber works on the pipes in your house, the water service and the sewer lateral. The 2024 storm water listing tells you the topic is on the city's radar. It does not tell you whether your lot has a drainage problem.

If groundwater reaches your crawlspace or basement, sump pump service is the plumbing side of the answer. Call Kentucky 811 before any digging, even for a drainage trench.

Signs worth a call in any established neighborhood

  • The same drain clogs more than once in a year.
  • A toilet gurgles when the washer drains.
  • Water stains appear on a ceiling or around the water heater.
  • The water bill rises with no change in use.
  • Rust-colored water does not clear after running the tap for a minute.
  • The main shutoff will not turn all the way.
