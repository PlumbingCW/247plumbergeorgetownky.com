Sewer Camera Inspection Cost in Georgetown, KY: What Counts

By the Georgetown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 15, 2026 · Drains and sewer

24/7 emergency line
(855) 575-2890Tap the number to call

A sewer camera inspection in Georgetown, KY costs more or less depending on five things. They are access for the camera, line length, cleaning first, locating the pipe above ground, and the records you receive. A sewer camera inspection means feeding a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through your house sewer to see its true condition.

We keep this page to factors instead of numbers, since a price quoted without seeing your cleanout would be a guess. If you are buying a house or chasing a slow drain, our sewer inspection service explains what the video should show and what you should walk away with.

Access and cleanout: the first price driver

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the house sewer. When one sits outside or in a basement and the cap turns, the camera goes in within minutes. When there is no cleanout, the plumber has to find another way in, which may mean pulling a toilet.

A missing cleanout is also a finding. If the line has none, adding one can make every later cleaning or inspection easier and cheaper. Ask the plumber to note where access exists and where it does not.

What happens during the visit

The process is the same for a quick slow-drain check and a pre-purchase inspection.

  1. Find access

    The plumber looks for a cleanout, then removes the cap or a fixture to open the line.

  2. Clean if needed

    Grease, roots or debris that block the lens are cleared so the camera can pass.

  3. Scan the line

    The camera travels toward the street connection while a monitor shows pipe walls, joints and any sagging.

  4. Locate and mark

    If a problem is found, a locator marks its depth and position above ground.

  5. Review the findings

    You see the video and hear whether the line needs cleaning, spot repair or replacement.

What to have ready before the camera arrives

A little preparation shortens the visit and keeps the scope clear.

  • Clear the area around the cleanout or the basement bath.
  • Note which drains are slow and which are fine.
  • Write down any past backups, and their dates.
  • Tell the plumber if roots, grease or flushed wipes have been an issue.
  • Ask whether the video will be saved and shared.
  • Find out whether your lot connects to a public main or a private line.
  • Decide in advance who needs the report, such as a buyer or an insurer.

Why cleaning first is part of the cost, not an extra

A camera cannot see through grease, roots or a mat of paper. If the lens clouds or the cable stops, the plumber must clear the line before the picture is useful. Think of the cleaning as part of getting a real answer.

Our sewer line cleaning page describes what that step involves and when the line may need more than a rodding. If the camera then shows damage, a sewer line repair option may follow, and your video helps decide which type.

Camera inspection versus guessing

Some owners skip the camera and clean the line again. That can work, but it leaves questions unanswered.

Camera first

  • Shows roots, cracks, offsets and sags.
  • Gives a recording for repairs or a home purchase.
  • Confirms where the problem sits and how deep.
  • Can avoid digging in the wrong spot.

Cleaning only

  • Clears the symptom for now.
  • Does not show pipe condition.
  • May miss a belly or a broken joint.
  • Repeat clogs can mean repeat visits.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call if drains in several fixtures run slowly, if you smell sewage, or before you close on a house with an older sewer. Sewage backing up into a floor drain is urgent, so stop using water and call. Georgetown, KY local note: public main, house sewer and the ground below

Georgetown Municipal Water and Sewer Service, known as GMWSS, provides sewer service in Georgetown. The utility cares about the public main, and your plumber cares about the house sewer that connects to it. Before any repair, call GMWSS and find out where the public main ends, so everyone knows which section is whose.

The Kentucky Geological Survey explains that Georgetown sits in the Bluegrass limestone region, where karst terrain lets surface water and pollutants move underground without filtering. Sewage entering karst is not filtered. A cracked or leaking house sewer is therefore more than a nuisance, and a camera finding should be repaired rather than ignored.

Replacing a house sewer needs a plumbing permit under the state plumbing regulation, 815 KAR 20:050, while cleaning out waste or sewer pipes does not. If the camera shows a failed section, your plumber should explain the permit step before any digging, and Kentucky 811 should be called before the ground is opened.
