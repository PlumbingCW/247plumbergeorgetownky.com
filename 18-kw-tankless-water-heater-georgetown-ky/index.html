Is an 18 kW Electric Tankless Water Heater Enough for Your Georgetown, KY Home?

By the Georgetown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 15, 2026 · Water heaters

24/7 emergency line
(855) 575-2890Tap the number to call

An 18 kW electric tankless water heater can supply hot water for a small to mid-size household, but it will not run every fixture at once in cold weather. The kilowatt number tells you how much heat the unit can add each second, and that heat is shared between how much water flows and how many degrees it must rise. For a home in Georgetown, KY, the winter inlet temperature is usually the limiting factor.

An electric tankless water heater is a wall-mounted unit that heats water only while a tap is open, using electric elements and no storage tank. This post explains how to read the 18 kW rating, how flow and temperature rise trade off, and what to ask an installer before you buy.

What 18 kW actually means

A kilowatt is a measure of power. An 18 kW unit can convert up to 18 kilowatts of electricity into heat while water flows through it. It does not store anything, so every gallon you use is heated on demand, and the heater works hardest when the water entering is cold.

That power draw is large for a household circuit. As raw arithmetic, current equals watts divided by volts, and 18,000 watts on a 240-volt supply works out to about 75 amps, before any safety margin. The actual circuit, breaker and wire size for a given model comes from the maker's installation manual, so ask the installer for the exact circuit requirement from the manufacturer's manual and a licensed electrician to confirm your panel can carry it.

Flow versus temperature rise

Temperature rise is the number of degrees the heater adds to the incoming water. With fixed power, more flow means less rise. If you open a second shower, each stream gets a smaller share of the heat, and the water coming out gets cooler.

Manufacturers publish charts that show the flow rate an 18 kW unit supports at different rises. Read those charts using your coldest inlet temperature, not an average. The point is a hot shower on a January morning, not a pleasant one in April.

Why winter changes the answer

Water in the buried service line comes into the house colder in winter, and the Lexington climate normals show roughly 90 days a year with lows at or below freezing. A tankless unit that handles summer flow easily may deliver warm, not hot, water when the inlet is at its coldest.

If you want one answer for the whole year, size for the cold month. Ask the installer what flow the unit sustains at your winter inlet, then compare it to the sum of fixtures you actually run together.

Scale on electric elements

Electric tankless units pass hard water across heating elements, and minerals in the water build up there as scale.

What scale does

  • Coats elements so they heat less efficiently.
  • Can trigger error codes or reduced output over time.
  • Restricts small passages inside the heat exchanger.
  • May shorten the life of a unit that is never serviced.

What helps

  • Testing your tap water before choosing a unit.
  • Considering a water softener or scale-control device upstream.
  • Scheduling periodic descaling, as the manual directs.
  • Cleaning inlet screens when flow drops.

Questions to ask before you buy

Take this list to any installer, including us.

  • Which exact model, and what is its listed flow at my winter inlet temperature?
  • What circuit does the manufacturer's manual call for?
  • Does my electrical panel have room for it?
  • Who pulls the plumbing permit?
  • What scale protection or descaling plan fits my water?
  • What happens to my old tank, vent and gas line, if I switch from gas?
  • Will a clear price be given before work starts?

Georgetown, KY local note: hard water and permits

GMWSS, the city utility, calls its water extremely hard because groundwater dissolves calcium and magnesium out of limestone on its way to the spring. The utility does not soften water at the plant. If you install an electric tankless unit, plan scale protection from day one and ask where the descaling ports will sit once the unit is on the wall.

No hardness figure appears on the utility pages we reviewed, so test your own tap or ask GMWSS before you rely on any assumption about your water.

Kentucky's plumbing regulation, 815 KAR Chapter 20, counts a new or replacement water heater as permit work, tankless included. A licensed master plumber can pull the permit, as can an owner-occupant working on their own residence. Ask who is pulling it and when the inspection happens.
