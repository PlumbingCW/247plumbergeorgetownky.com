Burst pipe right now: shut off the water in this order

Water damage grows by the minute, so these five actions come before any phone call to your insurer or a search for the break.

  1. Close the main valve

    It is usually where the line enters the house, in a basement, crawlspace, garage or utility closet. Turn a ball-valve lever a quarter turn, or turn a round gate-valve handle clockwise until it stops.

  2. Open a faucet or two

    Start with the lowest one in the house. This drains the lines and takes pressure off the broken pipe.

  3. Cut power or gas to a leaking heater

    If the water heater is wet or its connections are leaking, switch off its electric breaker or turn its gas control to off so it does not run dry.

  4. Stay clear of wet electrical areas

    Do not stand in water near outlets, appliances or the breaker panel. If water is falling through a light fixture, leave that room alone.

  5. Photograph everything

    Take pictures and short video of the break, the water and the damaged belongings before you clean up, for your insurance claim.

Cracked or broken pipe: what actually failed

A burst pipe is rarely a clean tear. It is usually a split along the pipe wall, a failed joint or a corroded spot that finally gave way. A lengthwise split from freezing looks like a thin slit, while corrosion shows as green or white crust and pinholes first.

The technician looks at how far the damage runs, because a pipe that failed in one place has often weakened elsewhere. If the damaged section is short and the rest of the line is sound, the repair replaces that section and joins it back with new fittings. If the pipe is thin, scaled or pitted along its length, patching only moves the failure down the line.

That decision is made with you before work starts, with the clear price attached. A line that keeps failing is the point to ask about replacing the whole run instead of paying for repeat visits.

Frozen pipe repair: thawing, splits and the Georgetown freeze season

Water expands as it turns to ice, and the pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet. That is where the pipe splits, but the leak often stays hidden until the ice melts and the water starts flowing again. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawlspaces, garages and attics freeze first.

If a pipe is frozen but not yet split, open the nearest faucet and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet side toward the blockage. Never use an open flame or a torch. If you see a split, a bulge or water, leave the thawing alone, close the main valve and call, because thawing a split pipe only releases the flood.

The repair is the same as for any burst pipe: cut out the split, replace the section and test under pressure. We also check nearby pipes that froze at the same time.

How many freezing nights Georgetown actually gets

The National Weather Service normals for Lexington, the nearest climate station, about a dozen miles south, put 89.9 days a year at or below 32 degrees. January averages 22.7 of them, and even November and December each see 11 or more.

Freezing nights also show up in October and April, so a pipe in an unheated garage can be at risk well outside midwinter. The practical lesson is to insulate exposed lines and know where your main valve is before the first hard freeze.

A supply valve that broke, seized or will not shut

Shutoff valves fail too. A stop valve under a sink or behind a toilet can crack or leak at the stem, and a main valve that has sat untouched for years can seize halfway. Do not force a stuck handle with a wrench, since a broken stem turns a slow leak into a spray.

If the main will not close, ask your water utility to shut the water off at the meter, using the number on your bill. Which utility reads the meter depends on the address, since the city and parts of northern Scott County are served by different ones. With the water off, the valve is replaced, giving you a main shutoff you can trust.

Pipe leaks and cracked drain pipes

A weeping joint or pinhole on a supply pipe is a smaller version of the same failure. It can be cut out and repaired the same visit, but a slow drip behind drywall still soaks insulation and framing, so treat it as urgent anyway.

A cracked drain pipe behaves differently because it leaks only when water runs through it. Stains below a bathroom, a musty smell or a wet spot that appears after a shower point to a drain, and a camera can confirm where it cracked. Underground sewer breaks belong on the sewer line repair page.

A burst pipe in Georgetown or Midway gets the same call-first response, and the service area page lists the other towns the line covers.

Permits: repairs do not need one, replacing a water service does

Kentucky's plumbing regulation, 815 KAR 20:050, lists repairing leaks, cocks or valves as work needing no permit. A new or replacement water service, a moved fixture or new plumbing installation does require one, pulled by a licensed master plumber or by an owner-occupant on their own residence.

So a split inside the house is a same-day repair. If the burst turns out to be on the buried line from the meter, replacing it becomes permit work with inspections before the trench closes, as covered under water service line repair.

What to check before you call

A few quick answers help the dispatcher plan the visit and send the right materials.

  • Is the main valve closed and has the water stopped?
  • Where is the water coming from: ceiling, wall, floor or yard?
  • Did the weather drop below freezing in the last day or two?
  • Is the damaged pipe copper, plastic or steel, as far as you can tell?
  • Is any water near electrical outlets, appliances or the panel?
  • Are there pictures of the damage for your insurer?
