Outdoor Faucet Repair in Georgetown, KY: Drips, Leaks and Freeze Damage

By the Georgetown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 15, 2026 · Winter

Outdoor faucet repair in Georgetown, KY starts with one question: where is the water coming from? A hose bib that drips at the handle, drips from the spout or leaks inside the wall each has a different cause and a different fix. The wall leak is the one to take seriously, because it can run for weeks before anyone sees it.

A hose bib is the outdoor spigot where you attach a garden hose, and a frost-free sillcock is the version built with a long stem so the shutoff sits inside the warm part of the house. This guide sorts the leaks by location, then covers how to keep the faucet from freezing and when a plumber should look at it.

Where the water shows up tells you what failed

Turn the hose bib on, then off, and watch carefully. Compare what you see with this table.

What you seeLikely causeTypical next step
Drip or spray around the handle when the water is onWorn packing behind the handleSnug the packing nut slightly, or replace the packing.
Steady drip from the spout when the faucet is offWorn washer or seat inside the valveReplace the washer, stem or the whole valve.
Water running down the wall inside or below the faucetSplit pipe or failed joint behind the wallShut off supply and call a plumber.
Spray from the vacuum breaker cap when runningNormal operation, or a worn breakerReplace the breaker if it sprays constantly.
No water or a trickle after a cold snapFrozen pipe near the faucetDo not force it, thaw gently and check for splits.

A drip of a few drops a minute adds up over weeks and shows up on your water bill, so a small leak is still worth fixing.

Why a hose left attached splits the pipe

Water expands as it freezes. A hose left on a hose bib holds water inside the faucet, so when it freezes the ice has nowhere to go and pushes back along the pipe. The pipe often splits inside the wall, where you cannot see it, and the damage only appears when you turn the water back on in spring.

A frost-free sillcock reduces that risk by placing the valve seat deep inside the wall. It still has to drain, which only happens if the hose is off and the faucet slopes slightly downward toward the outside. Check the installation instructions for your model, because a faucet that cannot drain is no better than an ordinary one.

Winterizing an outdoor faucet, step by step

These steps take a few minutes per faucet and can prevent a wall leak.

  1. Disconnect every hose

    Remove hoses, splitters and sprayers, and drain them before storing.

  2. Look for a shutoff inside

    Some homes have an interior valve for each outdoor faucet. Closing it and opening the outside tap to drain is the standard method.

  3. Open the outdoor faucet

    Leave it open so any water left in the line can escape. A covered faucet cap helps block cold wind.

  4. Check for drips in spring

    Turn the water on slowly and look at the wall around the faucet. Any moisture means the pipe may have split.

Repair the faucet or replace it?

Age and condition usually settle this question.

Repair when

  • The leak is only at the handle or spout.
  • The faucet body is sound and not corroded.
  • Replacement parts are available for the model.
  • There is no sign of water behind the wall.

Replace when

  • The valve has leaked after earlier repairs.
  • The body is cracked or heavily corroded.
  • The faucet is an old style without frost protection.
  • A freeze has already split the pipe.

Fall and spring faucet inspection

Walk the outside of the house twice a year with this list.

  • Disconnect and drain every hose before the first hard freeze.
  • Look for stains or soft spots on the siding under each faucet.
  • Check basement or crawlspace ceilings near the faucet for damp spots.
  • Open each faucet slowly in spring and watch for sprays or drips.
  • Confirm the faucet runs with full pressure.
  • Replace any faucet that drips from the spout and will not stop.

When to call a licensed plumber about an outdoor faucet

Call if water is showing inside the wall, in the basement under the faucet or along the foundation.
