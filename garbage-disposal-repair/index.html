A garbage disposal is a motor and a grinding plate mounted under the sink drain, with a seal at the flange, a drain elbow on the side and often a dishwasher hose on the inlet. Repair covers the motor, the seals and the connections. When the unit is worn out, replacing it is a short job, and when the sink itself will not drain, our drain cleaning service picks it up.

Jammed or leaking disposal right now: what to do first

These steps keep you safe and keep the damage small, day or night.

  1. Cut the power

    Turn the switch off, then unplug the unit or flip its breaker. A wall switch alone is not enough before anyone reaches near the unit.

  2. Never put a hand inside

    Use tongs or long pliers to lift out a spoon or bottle cap that you can see with a flashlight.

  3. Stop the water

    Run no more water into the sink, and skip the dishwasher, which drains through the disposal.

  4. Do not pour in drain cleaner

    Chemicals sit in a jammed unit and splash on whoever opens it next.

  5. Call for after-hours help

    We dispatch overnight and on weekends if the leak is active or the sink is full.

Disposal leaking under the sink

Dry the cabinet, run the unit with water, and watch where the first drop appears. A leak at the top ring points to the flange seal. A leak at the side points to the drain elbow gasket, and a leak at the very bottom usually means the housing has cracked. Reseating a seal or replacing a gasket is a simple repair.

A cracked housing cannot be patched. The unit is a one-piece cast or stainless shell, and once it leaks from the body, it needs to be replaced. Whatever the source, wet cabinet floors swell and grow mold quickly, so we also check the supply stop and the trap for slow drips while the cabinet is open.

Jammed disposal and the reset button

A jam happens when something hard wedges between the spinning plate and the shredding ring. Peach pits, bones, glass and utensils are the usual causes. Fibrous food such as celery or corn husks can also bind the plate. The motor hums, then trips its thermal overload to protect itself.

With the power off, most units have a hex socket in the center of the bottom of the housing. A hex key fits it, and working it back and forth frees the plate. After clearing, wait a few minutes and press the red reset button on the bottom. If it trips again right away, the motor or the start capacitor has failed, which is a repair for us rather than another try with the key.

Humming, grinding or silence from the motor

A hum means power is reaching the motor but the plate is not turning. Silence means no power or a dead motor. Grinding or rattling while it runs usually means a nut or spoon fragment is bouncing in the chamber. We test power at the switch and outlet first, then the reset, the plate and the motor. A burned-out motor is almost always cheaper to replace than rebuild.

Replacing a garbage disposal

Replacement means removing the old unit from the mounting ring, connecting the new drain elbow, and wiring or plugging it in. We reuse the sink flange when it is in good shape and replace it when it is cracked or has lost its seal. Mounting hardware and the splash guard are part of the job.

One step that is easy to miss: new units have a knockout plug in the dishwasher inlet. If it stays in, the dishwasher will not drain into the disposal. We remove it and check the hose and the high loop or air gap while the cabinet is open.

Kitchen drains in Georgetown's older neighborhoods

Georgetown has historic residential districts such as Lane's Run and South Broadway, and homes of that age may have older kitchen drain lines. Old lines can be rougher inside or smaller in diameter, so grease and fibrous scraps settle there faster than in a newer subdivision.

Ask GMWSS what it advises about grease and food waste before you rely on the disposal for heavy scraps. We do not claim a local rule here, but running cold water and avoiding grease are sound habits, and the sewer lines in your home are our shared concern. If the kitchen line keeps slowing down, a camera look through sewer inspection can show the cause.

What to check before you call about a disposal

These simple checks help us arrive with the right unit and parts.

  • Note whether the unit hums, clicks or is silent when switched on.
  • Check that the breaker has not tripped and the outlet has power.
  • Look for the red reset button on the bottom and see if it has popped.
  • Dry the cabinet and see where water first reappears.
  • Find the brand and horsepower on the label if you can read it.
  • Know whether a dishwasher drains into this unit.
