Downtown Georgetown, KY is the oldest part of the city, and its blocks mix historic houses, storefront buildings and apartments within a few streets of each other. A plumbing problem here depends on which kind of building you are in. This page separates what is documented about the area from general plumbing knowledge and from what only an in-person look can answer.

If water is spreading right now, close the main valve first. Then call the line and give the street name and the type of building. Say if it is a single house, a unit in a larger building or an apartment above a shop.

We do not claim that older buildings have bad pipes. Age is a reason to inspect, not proof of a problem.

Three kinds of building, three kinds of call

Single houses on the residential streets usually have their own main shutoff and their own sewer connection. Their calls look like calls anywhere in town, such as a clogged line or a failed heater, and drain cleaning is the most common first step for a slow drain.

Apartments above storefronts are different. Water lines and drain stacks can serve more than one unit, and the person who can approve a repair may be the building owner. If a ceiling is stained below you or a neighbor reports water, say so on the call. A leak detection visit can trace where the water starts before anyone opens a wall.

Mixed-use buildings add a third question: does the problem sit on the residential side or the commercial side?

Calls that come up in older city blocks

These are common job types, not predictions about your building.

Drains that keep returning

A line that clogs again within weeks may have a cause deeper than the clog. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe instead of guessing.

Water where it should not be

Damp plaster, a stained ceiling or a high water bill can point to a hidden leak. Shut off the main if water is active.

Old shutoff valves

A valve that has sat untouched for years can stick or leak when turned. Do not force it. Say on the call that it will not move.

Before you call from a downtown address

  1. Find your main shutoff

    It is often near the meter or where the water line enters the building. In a shared building, ask the owner which valve serves your unit.

  2. Note the building type

    Say if you are in a house, an apartment or a mixed-use building. It changes who can approve the repair.

  3. Check for gas

    If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call a plumber.

Permits and historic status

Under 815 KAR 20:050, a new or replacement water heater needs a plumbing permit. The permit holder is a master plumber or an owner working on their own residence. Fixing a leak or clearing a drain does not need one.

National Register listing is an honor and does not by itself restrict what a private owner does inside a building. Whether the city adds its own review for exterior work is a question for the city, so ask before planning a change that shows from the street.
