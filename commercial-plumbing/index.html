After-hours leak or backup at a business: what to do first

The first half hour decides whether the damage is a mop job or a rebuild.

  1. Close the main water valve

    Know where it is before you need it. If you rent, ask the property manager now and write the location on the wall.

  2. Cut power in wet areas

    Do not stand in water near panels, outlets or equipment. Turn off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

  3. Protect stock and records

    Lift boxes, paper files and electronics off the floor, and place buckets or tarps under active drips.

  4. Call and tell us who authorizes the work

    Evening, night and weekend dispatch runs all week. Give us the address, a contact on site and who approves the invoice.

Commercial leak detection: what is realistic

Finding a leak in a commercial building is harder than it sounds. Pipes run through ceilings, slabs and walls that belong to other tenants, and a meter that keeps moving with everything shut off might be fed by several lines. We use a meter test, listening equipment and pressure checks to narrow down where the water is going.

Be aware of what this service is and is not. A finding tells you where the problem is, but repair may require the owner to approve opening a wall, a tenant to give access, or a restoration crew to dry materials first. Some leaks are in shared lines whose ownership is not obvious. We report what we find in plain language so the right party can decide. For a pipe that has already split, see burst pipe repair.

Backflow testing and backflow preventers

A backflow preventer keeps water that has been in your building, with whatever it picked up, from flowing backward into the public supply. Restaurants, medical offices, irrigation systems and fire lines often have one. Testing is done by a certified backflow tester, and your water utility sets the schedule, so ask GMWSS or the utility that serves your address what it expects.

We dispatch plumbers to repair or replace failed assemblies, and we coordinate with a certified tester where one is needed. Adding a backflow preventer to an existing water service requires a plumbing permit under the Kentucky regulation, which we pull through a licensed master plumber. We do not claim to certify test results ourselves.

Grease trap cleaning and kitchen drain lines

A grease trap or interceptor catches fat before it reaches the sewer. When it is full, grease passes through and hardens in downstream pipes, and the first sign is often a slow floor drain or an odor at the back of the kitchen. Pumping out a trap is normally done by a licensed hauler, not a plumber.

What we do is the plumbing around it: clearing the lines, checking the baffles and fittings, and replacing a cracked trap or failed cover. If your kitchen drains keep backing up despite regular pumping, we can run a camera to look for a belly or a heavy grease build-up. Ask the utility about its grease rules, since we will not guess them for you.

Storm drain and roof drain cleaning

Parking lots, loading docks and flat roofs shed water into storm drains and roof drains. Leaves, silt and trash block them, and a blocked roof drain can leave water standing on a flat roof or overflowing to a wall. Storm systems are separate from sanitary sewers and should never be used to dump wash water.

We can flush and clear private roof leaders and catch basins on your property. Public storm sewers belong to the city or county, and large basin cleaning may call for vacuum equipment we do not carry. Before digging near a drain line, Kentucky 811 must be called. If clearing does not solve a repeated flood, the problem is likely in the pipe's slope or a collapse.

Georgetown buildings, big and small

Georgetown's commercial buildings range from small shops along US 25 and Lexington Road to large plants of the kind Toyota Motor Manufacturing Kentucky, which opened in 1988, and institutions like Georgetown College or Georgetown Community Hospital. We cite them only as examples of building types, not as clients.

Larger buildings have many zones, valves and floor drains, and a leak can be a long way from the cause. Before an emergency, find the valve map and mark which shutoff serves which wing. A tenant space in a strip center may share a line with its neighbor, so a quick call to the property manager saves time on the night something fails.

Information that speeds up a commercial service call

Property managers and owners who have these ready spend less time on the phone.

  • Street address and the suite or unit number.
  • A name and number for someone on site who can let us in.
  • Who approves the work and who receives the invoice.
  • Location of the main water valve and any floor shutoffs.
  • Whether the building has a grease trap, ejector pit or backflow device.
  • Photos of the leak, stain or backed-up drain.
