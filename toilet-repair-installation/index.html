What to check before you call about a toilet

A few minutes of looking can tell the technician what to bring and may stop water damage while you wait.

  • Find the stop valve on the wall or floor behind the bowl and confirm it turns.
  • Lift the tank lid and see whether water is spilling into the tall overflow tube.
  • Press on the bowl and tank. Any rocking means loose bolts or a failing seal.
  • Look for damp flooring, soft vinyl or a stain on the ceiling below.
  • Note whether the problem is only in this toilet or in other drains too.

Toilet repair: fill valves, flappers, handles and seals

Most toilet repairs happen inside the tank. The fill valve refills the tank after each flush and shuts off when the float rises. If it sticks or leaks, water keeps trickling into the overflow tube and the toilet hisses or restarts by itself. The flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. When it warps, water creeps into the bowl and the fill valve tops the tank up again and again.

A weak flush is a different problem. Common causes are blocked rim jets, a chain that is too slack, or a partial clog further down the line. A handle that must be held down usually points to a loose or kinked chain. We work through these in order, starting with the cheapest fix, and you hear the price before the work begins.

Replacing a fill valve or flapper is routine work and needs no permit, since the state plumbing regulation exempts repairing cocks and valves. If a tank has been patched several times, the toilet itself may be the problem.

Georgetown's hard water and what it leaves in the tank

GMWSS describes its water as extremely hard, from calcium and magnesium picked up as groundwater moves through limestone, and says it does not soften water at the plant. In a tank that mineral can crust on the fill valve seat, the flapper edge and the rim jets, which is why a flapper that looks fine can still leak.

Ask GMWSS or test your tap if you want to know how hard your own water is, because the utility does not publish a figure we can quote.

Running toilet or a puddle around the base

To test a running toilet, add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. A toilet that cycles on its own at odd hours, or a water bill that climbs with no change in habits, usually has this fault.

Water around the base is a separate matter. It can come from the supply line, the tank bolts or a failed wax ring. If the floor gets wet only after a flush, the wax ring is the likely cause. Do not wait on it. Moisture under a toilet soaks into the subfloor and can reach the ceiling below.

Toilet installation and replacement

A replacement starts with the old toilet coming up and the closet flange being inspected. A bent or rusted flange, or soft subfloor around it, has to be fixed first or the new toilet will rock. Then a new wax ring or wax-free seal goes down, the bowl is set, and the closet bolts are tightened evenly. Uneven tightening is how porcelain cracks.

Measure before you shop. Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, and most toilets are made for twelve inches, though some homes have ten or fourteen. Bowl shape, seat height and flush type matter too, and we can bring a model to the house that fits your space.

On permits, the Kentucky plumbing regulation under 815 KAR Chapter 20 requires one to move a toilet's waste opening. Repairs and valve work do not need one. Replacing a toilet in the same spot is not clearly addressed in the material we reviewed, so ask the state plumbing inspector before you start.

Overflowing toilet right now: what to do first

If the bowl is rising or water is on the floor, these steps limit the damage until a plumber arrives. They work at midnight as well as at noon.

  1. Stop the water

    Turn the stop valve behind the toilet clockwise. If it will not move, lift the tank float or close the main valve.

  2. Do not flush again

    Every flush sends more water into a bowl that cannot drain.

  3. Protect the room

    Put towels at the door and keep people away from outlets, fans and light fixtures that are wet.

  4. Call for dispatch

    Dispatch runs all night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.
