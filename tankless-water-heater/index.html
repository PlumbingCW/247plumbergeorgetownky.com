A tankless water heater warms water only while it flows, so it is quick to run out of range and quick to show the effects of scale. Georgetown 24/7 Plumbing installs, services, and flushes every tankless water heater type in Georgetown, KY, and across Scott County. A tankless water heater, also called an on-demand heater, heats incoming water through a heat exchanger instead of storing a hot tank.

If yours shows an error code, goes lukewarm, or sends a burst of cold in the middle of a shower, the cause is often fixable. A failed system can also reach us through 24/7 dispatch. Tankless flush and descaling

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger to dissolve mineral buildup. We close the isolation valves, connect hoses and a pump to the service ports, run the solution for the time the manufacturer specifies, then rinse and restore the valves. The inlet screen is cleaned in the same visit.

Scale acts like insulation on the heat exchanger. The unit works harder, the exchanger runs hotter, and sensors can trip codes before anything has really failed. In hard water, the interval between flushes is shorter than the label suggests, so follow the manufacturer's schedule and check your water.

A flush is maintenance, not a repair. If the exchanger is already cracked, scorched, or leaking, a flush will not fix it, and we tell you plainly. Units without service valves need them added before a proper flush can happen.

Tankless water heater sizing and installation facts

A tankless unit is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat by a given temperature rise. The number that matters is how many fixtures run at once. A shower and a dishwasher together may ask for more than one small unit can give, which is where the lukewarm complaint begins.

Gas units usually need a larger gas supply than a storage tank and their own venting. Electric units draw heavy current, so the panel must have the capacity. Columbia Gas of Kentucky holds the natural gas franchise in Georgetown, so questions about the meter belong with that utility. A tankless install is permit work under the state plumbing code, so the master plumber handles that step.

Where the old tank sat on the floor, the new unit mounts to the wall. That frees space, though the gas, water, and vent connections all move. Planning that on paper first avoids surprise costs.

Why Georgetown's water is hard on tankless heat exchangers

The Georgetown Municipal Water and Sewer Service calls its water extremely hard, because the groundwater collects calcium and magnesium from limestone. Softening at the plant is not part of its treatment, so the minerals reach your heater untouched.

A tankless heat exchanger has narrow passages and runs hot. Scale forms there quickly, and the first sign is often an error code or lukewarm output. A test at your own tap tells you where you stand, and GMWSS can answer questions about its supply.

Softener, scale inhibitor, or filter: what each does for a tankless heater

People bundle all three under water treatment. They do different jobs, and none replaces periodic flushing. We test your water and advise before we recommend anything.

Softener and scale inhibitor

  • A whole-house softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium, so less scale forms.
  • A scale inhibitor changes how minerals form, but does not remove them.
  • Neither removes chloramine, and softeners need salt, space, and a drain.
  • Check resin and inhibitor instructions for disinfectant limits.
  • Both reduce scale but flushing is still advised.

Point-of-use or whole-house filter

  • Filters target taste, sediment, or chlorine compounds, not hardness.
  • Georgetown's water is disinfected with chloramine, which basic carbon removes poorly.
  • Catalytic carbon media are made for chloramine, so ask before buying.
  • A filter on the heater inlet can catch grit, not scale.
  • A drinking-water filter at one tap does nothing for the heater.

Water softener and filtration trade-offs for a tankless home

Softened water protects the heat exchanger, but it adds a tank, salt or potassium to buy, and a drain connection. It also makes some people dislike the feel of the water. We do not push a softener on every home, and we say when a smaller step, such as a scale inhibitor, suits the house better.

GMWSS uses chloramine as its disinfectant, and the utility publishes that in its annual water quality report. Chloramine affects what a filter cartridge can do and how long softener resin lasts, so match the media to it. If you want to know what is in your tap water, the report and a lab test are the right starting points.

On the visit we look at the heater, the water pressure, and any treatment already installed. Then we explain what each option would change and what it would not. You decide, with a clear price before work starts.

Symptom checklist before you call

A few notes help us bring the right parts and tools.

  • Write down the brand, model, and any error code on the display.
  • Note whether the cold burst happens on short draws or long ones.
  • Check that the gas valve and water valves are open.
  • Check the inlet screen only if the manufacturer's guide shows how.
  • Note when the unit was last flushed, if ever.
  • Tell us if the unit has isolation valves for flushing.
  • Gas odor? Go outside and phone the gas utility's emergency number or 911 before anything else.
