Safety first: when 911 comes before us

Call 911 first if anyone is hurt. When you smell gas, get out of the building right away and ring Columbia Gas of Kentucky's emergency line, or 911, once you are outside. Leave the lights alone and hold off on calling any plumber until you are somewhere safe.

Water near the electrical panel is the other case that puts safety ahead of repair. Stay out of the room and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.

What to have ready before you dial

A short call is a faster call. These details help dispatch decide how urgent the problem is.

  • The street address, including the nearest cross street.
  • What is leaking, backing up or not working.
  • Where the water is going and how fast.
  • Whether you have found and closed the main shutoff.
  • Whether anyone is hurt or you smell gas.
  • Whether the property is a house, rental or business.
  • A good number to reach you if the call drops.

What happens after the call

Here is the sequence, from your side of the phone.

  1. You get instructions

    We tell you how to limit damage while you wait, such as closing the main valve or draining pipes.

  2. A plumber is assigned

    Dispatch sends a licensed plumber as soon as one is free. We do not promise a fixed arrival window.

  3. You hear the price

    After the plumber has looked at the problem, you get a clear price before work starts.

  4. Work begins only when you agree

    If you decline, you decline. If you agree, repairs start and the plumber explains what was done.

Know your shutoff before the night you need it

The main valve is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Find it now, turn it once to make sure it moves, and tell everyone in the house where it is.

If it will not turn, say so when you call. We will tell you what else you can do while a plumber is on the way.
