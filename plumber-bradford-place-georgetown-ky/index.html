What homes from the 2000s tend to ask for

A house that is twenty years old is not old for plumbing, but its small parts have had twenty years of use. The usual calls are a water heater near the end of its life, a toilet that runs after flushing and a faucet that drips at the handle.

Each of those is a modest job when it is caught early and a larger one when it is not. Water heater replacement is a planned job if you act before the tank leaks. Faucet repair and toilet repair are usually quick visits.

A ten-minute mid-life checkup you can do yourself

None of this needs tools. Write down anything that looks wrong and mention it on the call.

  • Read the label on your water heater for the manufacture date.
  • Look for rust or water at the base of the tank.
  • Check under every sink for corrosion or damp cabinet floors.
  • Put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank and watch the bowl for color without flushing.
  • Turn the main shutoff a quarter turn and back to confirm it moves.
  • Find your sewer cleanout, usually a capped pipe outside near the house.

Cleanouts, drains and why access matters

Many homes from this period have an outdoor cleanout cap on the sewer line. If a main drain backs up, a plumber can reach the line through that cap. Knowing where it is saves time on the call.

A sewer camera inspection is worth considering when a drain backs up repeatedly, because it shows whether the problem is a clog or damage to the pipe.

Permit and utility notes

Kentucky's plumbing regulation treats a water heater swap as permit work, so expect the plumber to pull one. Leak repairs and drain cleaning fall outside that requirement. The city utility serves most of Georgetown, and the service area page covers the exceptions.
