How to Plumb a Bathroom in Georgetown, KY: Rough-In Overview

By the Georgetown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 15, 2026 · Installation

24/7 emergency line
(855) 575-2890Tap the number to call

To plumb a bathroom in Georgetown, KY, you lay out the fixtures, run supply lines for water in, run drain and vent lines for waste out, test everything, and pass inspection before any wall is closed. That sequence is called the rough-in. It happens after framing and before drywall, and the order matters because pipes that are buried cannot be inspected.

This is an overview for homeowners who want to understand the work, plan a remodel or judge a contractor. It leaves out step-by-step cutting and joining on purpose. If your project needs a licensed hand, our plumbing installation team can handle the rough-in while you keep control of the layout.

The rough-in in five stages

Every bathroom follows the same basic order, whether it is a half bath or a full suite.

  1. Plan the layout

    Mark the toilet, sink, tub or shower on the floor plan and note which walls hold the pipes. Grouping fixtures on one wall shortens every run.

  2. Run the drain and vent lines

    Waste pipes go in first because they depend on gravity. Each fixture drains downhill and connects to a vent.

  3. Run the supply lines

    Hot and cold lines are then routed to each fixture, with a shutoff valve planned at each one.

  4. Test the system

    The installer closes off the lines and checks them under pressure or water to find leaks while the framing is still open.

  5. Inspect, then close the walls

    Inspection comes before concealment. Only after approval do insulation and drywall go up.

Supply lines versus drain, waste and vent: the two systems in your walls

A bathroom contains two separate plumbing systems that never mix. Knowing the difference helps you read a plan or a bid.

Supply side

Pressurized hot and cold water travels in small pipes to each fixture. A leak here shows up as a wet spot even when nothing is running.

Drain and waste side

Used water leaves by gravity in larger pipes. These lines are not under pressure, so they rely on slope instead.

Vent side

Vent pipes let air into the drain system so water can flow without gurgling or siphoning the traps dry. They typically run up through the roof.

P-traps

Each fixture drain has a curved trap that holds water to block sewer gas from entering the room.

Shutoff valves

Small valves at fixtures let you isolate one sink or toilet. The main shutoff stops water to the whole house.

Cleanouts

Access points in the drain system allow a line to be cleared later without opening a wall.

Why slope and venting cannot be improvised

A drain pipe that is too flat lets solids settle, and one that is too steep can let water run past waste and leave it behind. The code sets the allowed slope, so check the current requirement with the state plumbing inspector instead of trusting a number from a video.

Venting is the part homeowners most often misunderstand. Without a vent, draining water pulls air through the nearest trap and empties it, which opens a path for sewer gas. A fixture can work for a while without a proper vent, then start gurgling or smelling after the next change in the house.

The same caution applies to joining mixed materials, setting pipe supports and sizing lines. These details are why a rough-in that looks fine can still fail inspection.

Homeowner permit or licensed plumber: what each path means

Kentucky allows two routes to a permitted bathroom. They carry different responsibilities.

Homeowner permit

  • Applies only to the home you occupy or are building as your residence.
  • You do the work personally and submit a riser diagram.
  • You must be present at inspections.
  • Future replacements still need inspection.

Licensed master plumber

  • The plumber pulls the permit and is responsible for the work.
  • Journeyman plumbers work under the master's permit.
  • You avoid the five-year homeowner permit bar.
  • Often easier when the layout is complex or access is tight.

Before you close any wall in a bathroom

Walk through this list with whoever is doing the work. Missing one item can mean opening finished walls.

  • Confirm the permit is issued and posted for the job.
  • Mark where each shutoff valve will be reachable later.
  • Check that every fixture has a drain, a trap and a vent.
  • Make sure the pressure or water test was completed and passed.
  • Photograph the open walls with pipes visible for your records.
  • Schedule the inspection before insulation or drywall is ordered.
  • Keep the inspection approval with your house records.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a professional if the work touches the main drain, requires moving a fixture, or involves a wall you cannot open safely. Anything involving gas lines or 120/240 volt wiring belongs to licensed trades, so a water heater or a heated floor is not a casual add-on. Georgetown, KY local note: the homeowner affidavit

The Kentucky Division of Plumbing within the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction publishes a homeowner affidavit for people who plumb their own home. It says you do the work personally on a home you occupy or are building for your own residential use. Rentals are not covered. If you plan to do this on a property you will sell or rent, hire a licensed plumber.

You submit a riser diagram, and inspections must happen before the work is concealed, with you present. A final inspection comes before anyone occupies the space, and later replacements need inspection too. A homeowner who takes this path is barred from another homeowner permit for a new home for five years, so think about future projects before you sign.

Hiring unlicensed people to do the work can lead to prosecution under KRS 318, with a penalty of up to $100 and 90 days listed in the affidavit materials. Ask any helper for the master plumber's Kentucky license and permit, and verify it with the state division.
