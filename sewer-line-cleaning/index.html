Sewer line cleaning in Georgetown, KY clears the main pipe that carries all of your house's wastewater out to the public sewer. When this line stops, more than one fixture fails at the same time, and sewage can rise in the lowest tub or floor drain. A 24/7 dispatch line is open for that situation. While you wait, avoid flushing, laundry and dishwashing, and keep everyone away from any room with water on the floor.

This page is about the main line, not a single sink. For one fixture that drains slowly, fixture drain cleaning is the right page. The cleaning methods here, cabling, root cutting and jetting, are the same family of tools, but they work on a larger pipe farther from the house.

Main line blocked: several fixtures backing up at once

The main line gathers every branch in the house. When it is blocked, water from a washing machine, a shower and a toilet all compete for one exit. The weakest, lowest opening shows it first, which is why a basement floor drain or a ground-floor tub often fills before anything upstairs.

Typical signs include a toilet that bubbles when you run the sink, water that rises in the tub after a flush, and sewer odor near a cleanout. If every fixture drains slowly at the same time, the blockage is almost always past the branches. A cable on a bathroom sink will not fix this.

Right now: what to do before the plumber arrives

These steps limit damage and help dispatch send the right equipment.

  1. Stop adding water

    No flushing, laundry, dishwasher or long showers until the line is open.

  2. Protect the room

    Lift rugs and boxes off the floor and keep children and pets out.

  3. Avoid wet electrical areas

    Leave outlets and power strips in a wet basement alone.

  4. Find the cleanout

    Clear a path to the outdoor cleanout cap if you know where it is.

  5. Tell dispatch what you see

    Say which fixtures are affected and how fast the water is rising.

How the main line gets cleaned

The method depends on what blocks the pipe and how long the run is.

Cabling

A powered cable with a cutting or boring head opens a path through soft clogs and paper.

Root cutting

Blade heads on a cable slice through roots that have grown into the pipe.

Hydro jetting

High-pressure water scours grease and buildup from the pipe wall. See hydro jetting.

Camera follow-up

A look afterward shows whether the pipe is sound or needs more than cleaning.

Roots, grease or something else in the main

Roots follow moisture, and a hairline crack or loose joint lets them slip into the pipe. Inside, they thicken and snag whatever flows past. A cutter pass can keep the line open for a long stretch, though regrowth is likely while the opening stays.

Grease clogs form from fat that cools and sticks to the pipe wall, narrowing it over time. Flushed wipes, even those marked flushable, can tangle into a plug. A cable breaks through, and jetting removes what is left on the wall.

Georgetown, KY local note: where the public main begins

Georgetown Municipal Water and Sewer Service owns the public sewer main, and your house line connects to it somewhere between the building and the street. If you are unsure where the public main ends and the private line begins, ask GMWSS before arranging work on a stubborn blockage.

That call can also tell you whether the problem is on your side at all. If neighbors on the same street have backups, the cause may be farther along, so mention it when you phone dispatch.

A clogged main that keeps coming back

If the main clogs again soon after cleaning, the pipe may be damaged. A crack, an offset joint or a low spot can catch debris no matter how cleanly the line was cleaned. In that case a camera look is the next step, and repair may follow.

Before you call about a blocked main

A few observations help the technician plan.

  • List every fixture that is slow, gurgling or backing up.
  • Note whether the problem started suddenly or built up over days.
  • Check the lowest drain in the house for rising water.
  • Locate the outdoor cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation or yard.
  • Mention large trees near the sewer path.
  • Think back to recent flushed items, such as wipes or paper towels.
