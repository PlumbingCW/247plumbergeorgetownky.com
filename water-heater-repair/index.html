A cold shower or a wet floor around the tank is a problem for tonight, not next week. Georgetown 24/7 Plumbing offers 24/7 dispatch for water heater repair in Georgetown, KY, and across Scott County. Water heater repair means finding the failed part, such as an element, thermostat, burner, valve, or fitting, and fixing it while the tank itself is still sound.

While you wait for us, close the cold-water shutoff above the tank. Then switch the heater's breaker off, or turn its gas control to off. Keep people and pets away from standing water near electrical outlets. A leak from the steel shell cannot be repaired, and our replacement service covers that case. A leak from a fitting or valve often can be fixed.

No hot water right now: what to do first

Our dispatch runs overnight and on weekends, so a lukewarm tank at midnight is a reasonable reason to call. Before we arrive, look at the breaker panel for a tripped switch, and on a gas unit see whether the pilot or igniter is lit. If a breaker trips again right after you reset it, leave it off and tell us.

Smell gas near the heater? Get everyone outdoors first. From outside, phone Columbia Gas of Kentucky's emergency number or 911, and only then call us. A gas odor is never a repair-first problem.

Water around the heater: leak, condensation, or relief valve discharge?

A wet floor under a tank has four common sources, and only one of them means the tank is finished. Condensation beads on a cold tank or cold pipes in humid weather, drips for a while, then dries. Relief valve discharge happens when pressure or temperature climbs past the valve's setting. Fittings and valves can also weep at their threads. A rusted shell seeping near the bottom is the tank wearing out.

A simple test sorts them. Dry the floor, then wrap dry paper towel around the supply and outlet connections, the drain valve, and the end of the relief valve pipe. Wet paper at a connection points to a fitting we can usually repair. A steady drip from the relief pipe points to the valve or to excess pressure. Moisture that returns only on humid days is likely condensation.

A relief valve that discharges is a safety device doing its job, so never cap or plug it. The Kentucky homeowner water heater sheet calls for the discharge pipe to end within 2 inches of the floor when there is a floor drain, or within 4 inches of the ground when there is not. Repeated discharge in a closed system often means the heater needs an expansion tank, which absorbs the pressure rise as water heats.

If the water is spreading and you cannot find where it starts, the source may be a nearby line rather than the heater. That is a job for leak detection before anyone replaces a sound tank.

Gas, electric, or hybrid: how repairs differ

The failed part depends on how the unit makes heat. Each type has its own short list of usual suspects.

Gas burner and pilot

A dirty burner, weak igniter, or failed thermocouple stops the flame. These are small parts, and they are usually quick to swap.

Gas venting

A blocked or poorly sloped vent can send exhaust back into the room. We inspect it on every gas call because the fault is a safety issue.

Electric elements

A tank has an upper and a lower element. One burned out element gives lukewarm water or short supply, not always a cold tank.

Electric thermostats

A stuck thermostat or tripped high-limit control can leave a tank cold or overheated. The test needs a meter and a de-energized unit.

Hybrid heat pump parts

Hybrid units add a fan, air filter, condensate drain, and heat pump circuit on top of the backup elements. Clogged filters and drains are common.

Sealed-system limits

Refrigerant circuit faults on a hybrid are a manufacturer-level repair. We tell you plainly when replacement makes more sense than chasing a sealed-system fault.

Why Georgetown tanks collect scale

The Georgetown Municipal Water and Sewer Service describes its water as extremely hard, because groundwater picks up calcium and magnesium as it moves through limestone. The utility also says it does not soften water at the plant. That means the minerals arrive at your heater.

Practically, scale settles on the tank floor and coats electric elements and gas burner surfaces. You may hear popping or rumbling, get less hot water than the tank size suggests, or lose an element early. A scheduled tank flush, done to the manufacturer's instructions, slows that buildup. A water test at your own tap tells you how hard your supply is.

Symptom checklist before you call

These checks take a few minutes and help our dispatcher match the right parts to your truck.

  • Note the fuel: gas, electric, or a hybrid with a fan on top.
  • Find the rating plate and read the model and manufacture date.
  • Check whether the breaker is tripped, or the pilot or igniter is lit.
  • Look for water at the relief valve pipe, the drain valve, and the top fittings.
  • Listen for popping, rumbling, or hissing and note when it happens.
  • Run hot water at a sink and note whether it fades quickly or never warms.
  • Photograph any rust, scorch marks, or puddles for the technician.

Where repair stops and replacement starts

We repair when the part is the problem and the tank is sound. We recommend replacement when the shell leaks, when the unit has failed repeatedly, or when the repair would cost a large share of a new heater. You will hear the reason before any work begins.

State plumbing rules exempt the repair of leaks, cocks, and valves from permitting. A brand-new or swapped-in heater is a different matter, and our replacement page explains that step. If you live east of town, service around Midway runs from the same dispatch.
