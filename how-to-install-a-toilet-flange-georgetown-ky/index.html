How to Install a Toilet Flange: A Georgetown, KY Homeowner Guide

By the Georgetown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 15, 2026 · Fixtures

24/7 emergency line
(855) 575-2890Tap the number to call

A toilet flange is the ring that bolts your toilet to the floor and connects its base to the drain pipe below. If you live in Georgetown, KY and your toilet rocks, leaks at the base or lets in a sewer smell, a damaged flange is a likely cause. A cracked or loose flange can often be fixed with a repair ring, but a rotted floor underneath means the job is bigger.

This guide explains how to tell the difference, what the replacement process involves and where the do-it-yourself line sits. For sorting out a toilet that is already rocking or leaking, our toilet repair and installation service page covers what a plumber checks on site.

Four signs the flange has failed

A flange hides under the toilet, so you notice its failure through what the toilet does.

The toilet rocks

Movement when you sit or shift weight means the bolts, the flange or the floor beneath has loosened.

Water at the base

A wet ring after flushing points to a failed wax seal, and a bent flange is often why the seal will not hold.

A sewer smell

A good seal blocks sewer gas. A faint smell near the base that stays after cleaning suggests the seal is open.

A soft or stained floor

Spongy flooring or dark stains around the toilet mean water has been getting into the subfloor for a while.

Repair ring or full replacement flange?

The choice depends on what is wrong with the flange and what is under it.

A repair ring usually works when

  • The pipe part of the flange is intact and the floor is solid.
  • The top ring is cracked or has broken bolt slots.
  • The flange sits at a workable height above the finished floor.
  • There is no sign of rot or past leaking.

Replacement is the better call when

  • The flange is corroded, crushed or separated from the pipe.
  • The subfloor around it is soft, dark or crumbling.
  • The flange sits below the finished floor line.
  • The drain pipe itself is damaged and needs work.

The replacement process, in order

This is the sequence a competent installer follows. Read it to understand the job, even if you decide to hire it out.

  1. Shut off and empty

    Close the supply valve, flush, and sponge out the remaining water in the tank and bowl.

  2. Lift the toilet

    Disconnect the supply line, remove the nuts on the closet bolts and lift straight up. A toilet is heavy and awkward, so use two people.

  3. Inspect the flange and floor

    Scrape off the old wax, then press on the floor around the opening. Any softness means the subfloor needs repair before anything else.

  4. Set the new flange

    Fasten it so it sits on top of the finished floor, following the manufacturer's instructions for height and for the pipe material.

  5. Reset with a new seal

    Use a new wax ring and new closet bolts, lower the toilet straight down, and tighten the nuts a little at a time on each side.

Flange height, wax rings and closet bolts

Height is the detail that most often goes wrong. If the flange sits below the finished floor, the wax ring has to bridge a gap it was not made for, and the seal can fail within weeks. Most installation guidance has the flange resting on top of the finished floor, so check the instructions that came with your flange and ask an inspector if you are unsure.

Never reuse an old wax ring. Once compressed it will not reseal the same way. Closet bolts are the two bolts that slide into slots on the flange and hold the toilet down. Tighten the nuts evenly and stop when the toilet is firm, because over-tightening cracks the porcelain.

If you find black staining or a musty smell once the toilet is up, stop and photograph it. That is a sign of long-term moisture.

Before you lift the toilet

Ten minutes of preparation prevents most of the mess.

  • Shut off the supply valve and confirm it actually stops the flow.
  • Lay towels and a bucket nearby for the water left in the trap.
  • Check that you have a new wax ring, closet bolts and a putty knife.
  • Clear a spot to set the toilet down on cardboard, not on the floor.
  • Stuff a rag in the drain opening once the toilet is up to block sewer gas.
  • Photograph the floor before and after for your own records.

When to call a licensed plumber about a toilet flange

Call a plumber if the floor feels spongy, the flange has separated from the pipe, or water is coming through a ceiling below the bathroom. The last one points to a hidden leak, and our leak detection service exists to find where water is actually traveling. Also call if you are moving the toilet, or if a repaired seal still leaks.

Georgetown, KY local note: do you need a permit for a flange?

Under the Kentucky plumbing regulation, fixing leaks and working on cocks or valves does not need a permit. Moving a fixture or a waste opening does. That means resetting a toilet over its existing flange is unlikely to need one, while shifting the toilet to a new spot in the bathroom will.

Replacing a flange in the same place sits in a gray area, because the regulation does not clearly address it. Rather than guess, ask the state plumbing inspector for Scott County, whose office hours are listed at the Scott County Health Department, 300 East Washington Street. A short question there can save you from redoing work later.
