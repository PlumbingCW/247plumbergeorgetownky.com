What to do in the first minutes of a plumbing emergency

Speed helps, but order matters more. These five moves limit damage and keep everyone safe until help arrives.

  1. Stop the water

    Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. In many houses it sits near the water meter, a basement wall or the spot where the line enters the crawlspace.

  2. Check for electrical risk

    If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, keep out of the room unless the power is already off and your feet are dry. Never stand in water to reach a breaker.

  3. Smell gas? Leave first

    If you smell gas, leave the building and call Columbia Gas of Kentucky's emergency line or 911 from outside, before calling any plumber.

  4. Relieve the pressure

    Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house so the pipes drain. Turn off a water heater's power or gas control if the tank is leaking.

  5. Call the 24/7 line

    Say the address, what is leaking and where the shutoff is. Photograph the damage for your insurer after the water is under control.

What Georgetown's water does to pipes and heaters

Georgetown Municipal Water and Sewer Service, known as GMWSS, draws its main supply from Royal Spring, which it classifies as groundwater under the direct influence of surface water. It also buys treated surface water from the Frankfort Plant Board and from Kentucky American Water as needed. GMWSS calls the water extremely hard, because calcium and magnesium dissolve as groundwater moves through limestone, and it does not soften at the plant.

Hard water leaves scale on heating elements and inside tanks, which shortens heater life and dulls fixtures. If your hot water runs short or your tank rumbles, ask about water heater repair and a tank flush. We do not quote a hardness number, so test your tap or ask GMWSS directly.

Parts of northern Scott County, including the Moon Lake, Deer Run and Mallard Point subdivisions, are served by Kentucky American Water instead. A main break there on 17 November 2025 caused low pressure and a 36-hour boil water advisory. Know which utility serves your address before an emergency, not during one.

Freeze nights in the Bluegrass

The National Weather Service normals for Lexington, the nearest climate station, show how often temperatures drop to 32F or below. January averages 22.7 such days, February 19.2 and December 19.3, and the year totals 89.9.

Pipes in unheated garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls split first on such nights. Leave a faucet dripping, open the cabinet doors under sinks, and learn where your main valve is before the cold arrives.

Licensed work, permits and how to check any plumber

Plumbing here falls under Kentucky's state code, 815 KAR Chapter 20, and the licensing law in KRS 318. Four jobs usually trigger paperwork: new plumbing, a relocated fixture or waste opening, a replaced house sewer or water service, and any new or replacement water heater. Fixing a leak, a cock or a valve, or cleaning out a waste line, needs no permit.

The regulation lists a $50 base plus $14 for each added fixture or appliance on residential jobs, so confirm current fees with the state before you budget. Journeymen work under a master's permit, and only a master plumber or a resident homeowner may pull one.

To verify any plumber, ask which master plumber holds the Kentucky licence and pulled the permit, then check with the Kentucky Division of Plumbing at dhbc.ky.gov. Using an unlicensed worker can bring prosecution under KRS 318, and homeowner permits do not cover rentals. We print no licence numbers here, so verify us the same way you would anyone.

Plumbing services we handle around the clock

Most nighttime calls begin with water, so we start there. A pipe that has split calls for burst pipe repair, and a damp ceiling or a meter that moves with everything off may need leak detection before anyone cuts into a wall. When the leak is outside, a failed line from the street is a job for water service line repair.

Drains cause the other half of the emergencies. A single stubborn blockage may only need drain cleaning, while sewage backing into a tub points to main line cleaning or, if the pipe has collapsed, sewer line repair. Grease and roots that return every few months are good candidates for hydro jetting.

Hot water is the third big category. A tank that has failed may be fixed, or it may be time for a replacement heater, and we explain both options before you decide. Fixtures matter too: an overflowing bowl needs toilet repair, a dripping spout needs faucet repair, and a jammed unit under the sink needs garbage disposal repair.

Basements and shops have their own needs, from a pump that fails during a storm, covered under sump pump service, to a restaurant line that backs up, which is the work of commercial plumbing. The full list of jobs sits on our services page.
